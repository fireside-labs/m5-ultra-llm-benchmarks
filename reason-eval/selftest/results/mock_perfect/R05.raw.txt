## Variables

1. Demand and need in the catchment: population size, growth, age mix, disease burden and unmet need
2. Payer mix and reimbursement: Medicaid/uninsured share determines revenue per visit and financial sustainability
3. Access: travel time, public transit, parking, walkability and hours for the target population
4. Competition and cannibalization: competitor capacity nearby, and patients pulled from our own existing clinic/hospital rather than new patients
5. Workforce: ability to recruit and retain physicians, nurses and staff willing to work at that site
6. Regulatory and zoning approvals: zoning/permitted use, permitting, licensing and timelines for medical/imaging use
7. Total real-estate and build-out cost: rent, lease terms, renovation, imaging shielding and time to open
8. Referral and downstream flows: links to the hospital, specialists, imaging and lab; keeping patients in-network
9. Mission and equity: serving underserved neighborhoods, community benefit obligations of a non-profit, health disparities
10. Site physical suitability: size, layout, visibility, ADA accessibility, safety, signage and room for walk-in flow
11. Growth and flexibility: room to expand, future population shifts, lease flexibility/exit options
12. Hazard and continuity risks: flood zone, disaster exposure, utility reliability; also: Strategic fit and partnerships: employers, schools, community organizations, the system's network strategy and payer contracts (value-based care)

## Top 3 priorities

1. Demand and need in the catchment: population size, growth, age mix, disease burden and unmet need
2. Payer mix and reimbursement: Medicaid/uninsured share determines revenue per visit and financial sustainability
3. Access: travel time, public transit, parking, walkability and hours for the target population

## What would change my mind

- A finding that reverses the top priority.
- New cost data.
