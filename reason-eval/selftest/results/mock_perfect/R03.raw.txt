## Missed issues

1. Medicaid managed-care contracts forbid billing members for missed appointments (34% of patients, and the highest no-show group)
2. Two commercial contracts require 90 days' written notice to the plan before a new patient-facing fee; the memo's 60-day patient notice doesn't cover it
3. Text-only reminders won't reach many patients: 15% have no mobile number, 41% have no texting opt-in, and the templates are English-only while 22% prefer Spanish
4. Equity and access: fees and balance holds deter low-income patients (who no-show most), delaying care and pushing them to the ED or out of the practice
5. The fee doesn't address the actual causes of no-shows (transportation 38%, work/childcare 19%); fixes for those are missing
6. Revenue projections are overstated: Medicaid exempt, first miss waived, 31% collection rate on small charges, and lost patients (9% left after collections) cost more than the fee brings in
7. Double-booking makes chronic lateness worse: 61% of sessions already run 20+ minutes late, so both-show days cascade delays, wait times and provider burnout
8. Staff workload shifts rather than disappears: fee disputes, waiver requests, balance conversations at booking, opt-in collection and inbound calls from patients who miss texts
9. No measurement plan: no pilot, baseline or control, and no tracking of unintended effects (attrition, ED use, complaints, no-shows by payer)

## Looks like a problem but isn't

- Urgent or same-day sick visits could be blocked by the balance hold: handled (The memo states urgent and same-day sick visits are never held for balances.)
- There is no grace period or hardship waiver: handled (First miss each year is waived automatically and leads can waive for documented hardship.)
- Patients are not told about the policy in advance: handled (Mailed and posted 60 days ahead, and patients sign the updated financial policy.)
