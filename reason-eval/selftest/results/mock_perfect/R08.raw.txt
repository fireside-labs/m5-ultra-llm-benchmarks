## Case for

1. Focused individual productivity: fewer interruptions and more deep-work time
2. Talent pool and hiring: recruit beyond commuting distance and widen diversity of candidates
3. Time and wellbeing: no commute saves hours, money and stress; better work-life balance
4. Retention: flexibility is valued; mandating office days drives attrition, especially of top performers
5. Cost: less office space and real estate
6. Inclusion: caregivers, people with disabilities and those far from offices can participate fully

## Case against

1. Collaboration and innovation: spontaneous interactions, weak ties and cross-team idea flow decline
2. Mentoring and early-career development: juniors learn by observation and quick feedback; onboarding is harder
3. Culture, cohesion and trust erode; loneliness and isolation rise
4. Coordination costs: slower communication, more meetings and messages, time-zone and async friction
5. Management and accountability: harder to observe and evaluate work; risk of shirking or of invasive monitoring
6. Two-tier workforce: proximity bias favours those in the office; unequal home setups

## Cruxes

1. Does remote-by-default raise or lower productivity for this company's kinds of work, measured as output rather than activity, including team-level and long-run effects?
2. Can mentoring, onboarding and early-career growth be made to work remotely, or do juniors fall behind?
3. Does innovation and cross-team collaboration actually depend on in-person contact, or can deliberate gatherings substitute?
4. Values: how much weight to give employee autonomy and wellbeing versus organisational cohesion and control

## What evidence would change the answer

- Results from a time-limited pilot.

## My lean

I lean toward a phased trial, about 60% confident, conditional on the pilot results.
