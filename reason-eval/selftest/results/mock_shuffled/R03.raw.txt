## Missed issues

1. Single point of failure: one box runs both training and the paying customer's live endpoint; any failure or maintenance takes everything down (with next-business-day repair)
2. Utilization mismatch: 85% of jobs use 1-2 GPUs and only 3% use 8, so the interconnect advantage rarely matters while two boxes reduce queueing between users
3. Lead time: the 8-GPU server takes 16 weeks to arrive, but the paying pilot starts in 6 weeks (4-GPU servers ship in 3)
4. Power: the 8-GPU server draws ~10.2 kW but the rack has only 6.5 kW free (8 kW limit minus 1.5 kW), so it needs a second rack too
5. The Harrow contract requires dedicated hardware not shared with any other workload; one shared box for training and Harrow violates it, whereas one of two servers can be dedicated
6. Rent the rare 8-GPU fine-tune in the cloud (~30 h x $98 = ~$3k per quarter) instead of buying for it
7. Resale value: 4-GPU servers keep ~55% after 2 years vs ~40% for 8-GPU systems with fewer buyers
8. Capital flexibility: with 14 months of runway and a board request to keep capex flexible, buying one 4-GPU server now and a second later is possible only with Option B
