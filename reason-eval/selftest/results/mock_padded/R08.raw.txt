## Case for

1. Stakeholders should be consulted more broadly.
2. The timeline seems aggressive.
3. Communication with the team could be improved.
4. There may be risks that need further analysis.
5. Focused individual productivity: fewer interruptions and more deep-work time
6. Talent pool and hiring: recruit beyond commuting distance and widen diversity of candidates
7. Time and wellbeing: no commute saves hours, money and stress; better work-life balance
8. Retention: flexibility is valued; mandating office days drives attrition, especially of top performers
9. Cost: less office space and real estate
10. Inclusion: caregivers, people with disabilities and those far from offices can participate fully
11. Collaboration and innovation: spontaneous interactions, weak ties and cross-team idea flow decline
12. Mentoring and early-career development: juniors learn by observation and quick feedback; onboarding is harder
13. Culture, cohesion and trust erode; loneliness and isolation rise
14. Coordination costs: slower communication, more meetings and messages, time-zone and async friction
15. Management and accountability: harder to observe and evaluate work; risk of shirking or of invasive monitoring
16. Two-tier workforce: proximity bias favours those in the office; unequal home setups
17. Does remote-by-default raise or lower productivity for this company's kinds of work, measured as output rather than activity, including team-level and long-run effects?
18. Can mentoring, onboarding and early-career growth be made to work remotely, or do juniors fall behind?
19. Does innovation and cross-team collaboration actually depend on in-person contact, or can deliberate gatherings substitute?
20. Values: how much weight to give employee autonomy and wellbeing versus organisational cohesion and control

## Top 3 priorities

1. Stakeholders should be consulted more broadly.
2. The timeline seems aggressive.
3. Communication with the team could be improved.
