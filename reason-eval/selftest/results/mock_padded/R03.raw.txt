## Missed issues

1. Urgent or same-day sick visits could be blocked by the balance hold
2. There is no grace period or hardship waiver
3. Patients are not told about the policy in advance
4. Stakeholders should be consulted more broadly.
5. The timeline seems aggressive.
6. Communication with the team could be improved.
7. There may be risks that need further analysis.
8. Medicaid managed-care contracts forbid billing members for missed appointments (34% of patients, and the highest no-show group)
9. Two commercial contracts require 90 days' written notice to the plan before a new patient-facing fee; the memo's 60-day patient notice doesn't cover it
10. Text-only reminders won't reach many patients: 15% have no mobile number, 41% have no texting opt-in, and the templates are English-only while 22% prefer Spanish
11. Equity and access: fees and balance holds deter low-income patients (who no-show most), delaying care and pushing them to the ED or out of the practice
12. The fee doesn't address the actual causes of no-shows (transportation 38%, work/childcare 19%); fixes for those are missing
13. Revenue projections are overstated: Medicaid exempt, first miss waived, 31% collection rate on small charges, and lost patients (9% left after collections) cost more than the fee brings in
14. Double-booking makes chronic lateness worse: 61% of sessions already run 20+ minutes late, so both-show days cascade delays, wait times and provider burnout
15. Staff workload shifts rather than disappears: fee disputes, waiver requests, balance conversations at booking, opt-in collection and inbound calls from patients who miss texts
16. No measurement plan: no pilot, baseline or control, and no tracking of unintended effects (attrition, ED use, complaints, no-shows by payer)

## Top 3 priorities

1. Stakeholders should be consulted more broadly.
2. The timeline seems aggressive.
3. Communication with the team could be improved.
