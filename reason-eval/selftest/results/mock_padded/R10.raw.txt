## Estimate chain

1. Stakeholders should be consulted more broadly.
2. The timeline seems aggressive.
3. Communication with the team could be improved.
4. There may be risks that need further analysis.
5. EV count: households x vehicles per household x EV share (8-16%) gives ~33k-67k EVs
6. Home-charging access: 25-35% of EV owners lack home charging and depend on public fast charging
7. Session frequency: 200 mi/week x 70% from DC fast / 150 mi per session = ~0.9 sessions/week for drivers without home charging
8. Drivers with home charging still add substantial load: ~2 sessions/month x the majority of EVs (~16k sessions/week, comparable to the no-home group)
9. Ride-hail and delivery fleets: 1,500 EVs x 5 sessions/week = 7,500 sessions/week
10. Port throughput: 25% of 24 h = 6 h = 360 min / 40 min = ~9 sessions per port per day
11. Subtract the 46 existing ports
12. Budget arithmetic: $12M / $140k-$220k = ~55-86 ports, a small fraction of the need
13. Leverage other sources: private charging operators, utilities, grants, and cheaper Level 2 / workplace charging
14. Phase and target: put first ports where residents lack home charging; build in stages and revisit as adoption becomes clearer
