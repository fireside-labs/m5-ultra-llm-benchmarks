## Missed issues

1. Readmissions to other hospitals are not captured
2. Patients with multiple discharges may be double-counted
3. The groups may differ in age or sex
4. Stakeholders should be consulted more broadly.
5. The timeline seems aggressive.
6. Communication with the team could be improved.
7. There may be risks that need further analysis.
8. Simpson's paradox: the program does worse within each risk tier (5.0% vs 4.0%, 25% vs 22%); the overall gap comes from enrolling mostly low-risk patients
9. Selection bias: enrollment required answering the first call and agreeing, so the program group is systematically more reachable, engaged and healthier
10. Survivorship / per-protocol bias: 140 dropouts who didn't complete all 3 calls were removed from the program group only
11. Confounding by site: Northgate launched meds-to-beds in the same period and supplies 85% of program patients vs 40% of comparison
12. Small samples and no uncertainty: the Spanish-language '100% reduction' is 2/10 vs 0/9, and no confidence intervals are given anywhere
13. Base-rate neglect: with 13% prevalence, 90% sensitivity and 70% specificity, only about 31% of flagged patients are readmitted, not 90%
14. Metric definition change: from July, planned readmissions no longer count, and the program group is more concentrated after July (70% vs 50%)
15. The forecast double-counts the effect: a 50% reduction is assumed although the within-tier data show no benefit, and it is applied to all flagged patients

## Top 3 priorities

1. Stakeholders should be consulted more broadly.
2. The timeline seems aggressive.
3. Communication with the team could be improved.
