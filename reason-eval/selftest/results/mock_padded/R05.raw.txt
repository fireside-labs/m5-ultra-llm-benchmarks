## Variables

1. Stakeholders should be consulted more broadly.
2. The timeline seems aggressive.
3. Communication with the team could be improved.
4. There may be risks that need further analysis.
5. Demand and need in the catchment: population size, growth, age mix, disease burden and unmet need
6. Payer mix and reimbursement: Medicaid/uninsured share determines revenue per visit and financial sustainability
7. Access: travel time, public transit, parking, walkability and hours for the target population
8. Competition and cannibalization: competitor capacity nearby, and patients pulled from our own existing clinic/hospital rather than new patients
9. Workforce: ability to recruit and retain physicians, nurses and staff willing to work at that site
10. Regulatory and zoning approvals: zoning/permitted use, permitting, licensing and timelines for medical/imaging use
11. Total real-estate and build-out cost: rent, lease terms, renovation, imaging shielding and time to open
12. Referral and downstream flows: links to the hospital, specialists, imaging and lab; keeping patients in-network
13. Mission and equity: serving underserved neighborhoods, community benefit obligations of a non-profit, health disparities
14. Site physical suitability: size, layout, visibility, ADA accessibility, safety, signage and room for walk-in flow
15. Growth and flexibility: room to expand, future population shifts, lease flexibility/exit options
16. Hazard and continuity risks: flood zone, disaster exposure, utility reliability
17. Strategic fit and partnerships: employers, schools, community organizations, the system's network strategy and payer contracts (value-based care)
