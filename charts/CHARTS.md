# Charts

Regenerate everything: `python charts/make_charts.py` from the repo root (reads only the JSONL files in results/longctx/, results/mtp/ and results/replay/; PNGs are 2x). Test system: Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studio, 256 GB unified memory, macOS 27.0.1. llama.cpp 19e28a2 (Metal, flash attention on, batch/ubatch 2048); oMLX 0.7.0rc1. All numbers are medians across reps, temperature 0 unless noted.

**01_qwen_prefill_vs_depth.png** - Cold prefill speed for Qwen3.8-Flash-Next from 10k to 250k tokens (both engines final: 3 reps, quiet machine; oMLX Homebrew build, block cache on, MTP off). oMLX oQ8e leads at every depth: 1,714 vs 1,620 t/s at 10k (6%), and the gap widens as llama.cpp Q8_0 falls to 1,069 t/s at 250k while oMLX holds 1,365 t/s (28% ahead).

**02_qwen_decode_vs_depth.png** - Decode speed against context depth. llama.cpp loses 63% of its decode speed between 10k and 250k (47 → 17.5 t/s). oMLX (MTP off) stays at 51-56 t/s, so it is 2.9x faster at 250k after a cold prompt and 3.0x faster on warm agent steps. With MTP on, oMLX reaches 59 t/s at 250k (3.4x); the MTP-on line is still preliminary (1 rep, measured while model downloads were running).

**03_qwen_warm_vs_cold_ttft.png** - "Pay prefill once". On a log axis: time to first token for a cold prompt vs a warm agent step (the same conversation plus 64 new tokens). At 250k a cold prompt takes 186-234 s. A warm step takes 0.64 s on llama.cpp and 4.8 s on oMLX. oMLX's warm cost grows by about 17 ms per 1k tokens of context; llama.cpp's is nearly flat (~0.9 ms).

**04_deepseek_prefill_decode_vs_depth.png** - DeepSeek-V4-Flash cold prefill and decode against depth. llama.cpp runs the Vision-Exp model at UD-Q8_K_XL (preliminary, 1 rep, 10k-500k). oMLX is the kernel build running the 0731 model at oQ4e (1k-200k). oMLX is faster at every depth both engines ran: prefill 800 vs 562 t/s and decode 30.3 vs 24.6 t/s at 200k. Because the variant and quant differ, this compares engine plus quant, not the engine alone.

**05_deepseek_warm_ttft_vs_depth.png** - Warm agent-step TTFT for DeepSeek. llama.cpp stays at 0.5-1.4 s all the way to 500k. oMLX grows linearly by about 42 ms per 1k tokens (the fit goes through its 4k-200k points; 200k takes 9.2 s). A 32 GB hot cache reproduced the oMLX curve within noise. With the block cache off, every warm step re-prefills the whole prompt (97 s at 100k).

**06_cold_vs_warm_ttft_ratio.png** - How many times faster a warm step starts than a cold prompt of the same depth, per model and engine (log axis). llama.cpp's ratio keeps climbing: 364x for Qwen at 250k and 921x for DeepSeek at 500k (400k: 934 s cold vs 1.23 s warm). oMLX levels off around 27-38x because its warm-step cost grows with depth.

**07_mtp_dspark_speedup_by_task.png** - Decode speedup from speculative decoding on oMLX, by task. Qwen MTP at temperature 0 gives 1.4-2.3x, largest on json and reasoning and smallest on prose and the 8k summary. At the recommended sampling settings (temp 1.0, top-k 20, top-p 0.95) it gives 1.4-2.2x. DeepSeek-0731 DSpark gives 1.5x on code, reasoning and json, 1.2x on the summary and nothing on prose. DSpark output is byte-identical to DSpark off on every task. Qwen MTP output diverges from MTP off on every task except json.

**08_deepseek_what_each_fix_bought.png** - DeepSeek at 10k, one change at a time. Prefill: llama.cpp ubatch 512 gives 628 t/s, ubatch 2048 gives 827, oMLX Homebrew build 1,204 and oMLX kernel build 1,368 (2.2x in total). Decode: 32.0 t/s on llama.cpp and 35.5 on oMLX. The kernel build adds nothing to decode (34.8). DSpark lifts it to 42.5 t/s on 10k book text and 63.1 t/s on a short code prompt. The llama.cpp → oMLX step also switches from 8-bit Vision-Exp to 4-bit 0731.

**09_qwen_replay_engines.png** - The same 181 requests recorded from one Qwen agent session (context growing to ~200k tokens, up to 256 output tokens each, MTP off) replayed in order on both engines, each starting from an empty cache. oMLX finishes in 18.5 min, llama.cpp in 29.7 min. llama.cpp starts each request sooner (median TTFT at 150-200k: 1.6 vs 2.4 s), but oMLX decodes far faster (52 vs 22 t/s at 150-200k, as llama.cpp's decode falls from ~50 t/s with depth), and decode dominates. Three panels against prompt tokens: TTFT and decode speed per request (dots, with a 15-request rolling median) and cumulative elapsed time. Caveat: llama.cpp was sent ignore_eos and always produced 256 tokens; oMLX stopped early on 39 of 181 requests (42,892 vs 46,336 output tokens). At its own decode speed the missing tokens would add ~1 min, so oMLX would still finish in ~19.5 min.
