## Variables

1. **Whether a real compliance or contractual gap exists.** "The data is sensitive" only justifies the switch if the cloud vendor leaves some obligation unmet — the signed DPA suggests it may already be met. *Check:* map every customer contract, HIPAA/business-associate duty, and cyber-insurance requirement against the vendor's attestations, and ask your largest insurers directly whether on-prem processing is required or merely preferred.
2. **Quality parity on Northfold's actual tasks.** Summaries and letters feed real claim decisions, so a regression is a liability and complaint risk, and open-weight models vary widely on long medical-record summarisation. *Check:* build a gold set of 200–300 annotated records, blind-compare the current API against candidate open-weight models, and measure hallucination, omission, and error rates.
3. **Full TCO including peak sizing.** Owned GPUs must be sized for quarter-end peaks and idle the rest of the month, and capex, power, and redundancy land on Northfold instead of being amortised across the vendor's customer base. *Check:* build a 3-year TCO (GPU capex sized to peak throughput/latency, power, support, staffing) against the actual API invoice — which I don't have; my prior is that 40k docs/month is modest and spiky, making dedicated hardware hard to justify, but the invoice decides.
4. **Engineering capacity of the two-person team.** Today they maintain an API integration; self-hosting means serving infrastructure, model updates, evals, monitoring, and on-call — a step-change in load. *Check:* estimate annual FTE-hours for the self-hosted stack and compare with the team's spare capacity and other roadmap commitments.
5. **Peak-to-average volume ratio and turnaround SLAs.** The spikier the volume and the tighter the turnaround commitments, the more hardware you must own and the greater the queueing risk at quarter end. *Check:* pull daily volumes for the last 8 quarters, compute the peak/mean ratio, and write down the latency each document type requires.
6. **Residual vendor risk.** What actually remains with the cloud — vendor breach, sub-processors, retention, training use, jurisdiction — is the risk the CISO is really pointing at. *Check:* run a vendor security review covering retention and training terms, sub-processor list, breach history, encryption/key management, and data-region pinning.
7. **Northfold's own data-centre security maturity.** On-prem is only safer if Northfold's controls beat the vendor's; a 300-person company's DC is plausibly weaker than a hyperscaler's. *Check:* compare physical security, patching, segmentation, 24/7 monitoring, and audit certifications of Northfold's environment against the vendor's.
8. **Hybrid and redaction options.** Stripping identifiers before the cloud call, or running a small on-prem model for triage while the cloud drafts letters, may capture most of the risk reduction at a fraction of the cost. *Check:* pilot a redaction pipeline on real records and measure the share of identifiers removed and its error rate on medical text.
9. **Model improvement cadence.** Cloud APIs improve continuously at zero effort to you; a self-hosted model only improves when your two engineers redeploy it — a gap that compounds over years. *Check:* review the API's quality changes over the past 12 months and ask the team how often they could realistically refresh a self-hosted model.
10. **Migration and fallback risk.** A botched cutover during quarter-end is itself an operational incident with customer-visible consequences. *Check:* require a parallel-run plan with defined rollback triggers and cost the transition period.
11. **Contract timing and negotiating leverage.** Renewal dates and price benchmarks determine whether there is a cheap moment to act — and a credible self-host option alone may improve terms. *Check:* note the renewal date and benchmark current per-token pricing against comparable API offerings.
12. **Insurance and liability treatment.** Liability for a bad decision letter is the same either way, but cyber-insurance premiums and coverage can differ for self-hosted infrastructure. *Check:* ask the broker to price both scenarios.

## Top 3 priorities

1. **Whether a real compliance or contractual gap exists.** This is the crux: if the DPA and attestations already satisfy customers and regulators, the proposal solves a perceived problem at material cost; if a major insurer or regulator genuinely requires on-prem processing, it dominates everything else. Settle this before any technical work.
2. **Quality parity.** The model's output is Northfold's product; no security gain compensates for worse claim decisions, and if no open-weight model matches the API on your gold set, the debate ends.
3. **Full TCO with peak sizing and staffing.** With two engineers and quarter-end spikes, this is where self-hosting most often fails economically: hardware sized for peak that idles most of the month, plus an ops burden the team cannot carry.

## What would change my mind

- A top insurer representing a material share of volume contractually requires on-prem processing, or offers pricing/retention incentives for it → flips toward self-hosting or a hybrid.
- The blind eval shows an open-weight model matching or beating the API on Northfold's records → removes the strongest objection to moving; a significant quality gap confirms staying.
- The TCO comes out opposite to my prior — a large API invoice and a flat (not spiky) volume profile make dedicated GPUs economically rational; a small invoice with 5–10× quarter-end peaks kills the case.
- The vendor review finds training on customer data, weak retention or sub-processor terms, or a material breach history → tilts sharply toward exit, or at minimum redaction; conversely, zero-retention terms, customer-managed keys, and in-region processing largely neutralise the CISO's argument.
- The redaction pilot removes nearly all direct identifiers with low error → a hybrid keeps the cloud API while answering the sensitivity concern, and dominates both extremes.
