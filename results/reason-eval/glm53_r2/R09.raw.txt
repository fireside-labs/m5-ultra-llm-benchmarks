## First steps

1. **Build the hour-by-hour load picture.** Pull calls arriving per hour (including abandoned) × current AHT, against agents on seat, for every operating hour. *Why now:* every decision — hire, callback, shift design — hinges on whether this is a total-capacity shortfall, a peak problem, or a demand/AHT problem; the mix decides the fix. *Needs:* ACD logs + one analyst; 2–3 days.
2. **Start backfilling the 2 vacant positions today.** *Why now:* it's the only capacity lever that lands within the quarter, the positions already existed, and recruiting takes weeks. *Needs:* HR and the call center manager; requisitions on day 1, overtime or cross-trained floats as a bridge.
3. **Spend half a day on the floor with agents and supervisors.** *Why now:* AHT jumped 37% in March — the agents know where the time went (screens, logins, workarounds) and why 26% of calls get transferred; cheap fixes (training, macros, cheat sheets) may recover much of it. *Needs:* supervisor to arrange; you attend.
4. **Listen to 40–50 recent recordings across the top call types** and time the components: EHR navigation, identity verification, hold-for-transfer. *Why now:* quantifies how much of the 5.6-minute AHT is recoverable and how much transfer double-handling costs. *Needs:* QA/supervisor and the recording system; 1 week.
5. **Trace the refill-status wave upstream.** Ask clinic/pharmacy ops why 31% of calls are "where's my refill": turnaround times, notification gaps. *Why now:* it's the largest call type and probably a big share of the 18% repeat callers — the call center is absorbing a clinic-side delay. *Needs:* pharmacy/clinic ops lead; 1 week.
6. **Ship menu/routing quick wins.** Add an automated refill-status lookup (IVR or message queue), cut the 7-option menu around the top three reasons, route booking straight to schedulers. *Why now:* cuts transfers and deflects the #1 category with no new spend. *Needs:* phone system admin or a vendor ticket; 1–2 weeks.
7. **Deploy callback (b) as a stopgap, with eyes open.** *Why now:* it's cheap, fast, and stops the 21% abandonment bleed (abandoners call back, inflating load) while real fixes land — but tell the COO plainly: it changes where people wait, not how much work exists. *Needs:* phone vendor and procurement; this month.
8. **Fix the metric definition and stand up a weekly dashboard.** Define "wait" precisely (answered-only vs. all callers; average vs. "80% of callers under 2 minutes") and report wait by interval, abandonment, repeat rate, AHT, and transfer rate weekly. *Why now:* the 9-minute figure excludes abandoners, and callback could "improve" the number without helping patients — the COO and board must agree on an honest target now. *Needs:* analyst + 30 minutes of COO time.

## Measure first

1. **Offered load by interval** — calls per hour including abandoned, × AHT, vs. agents staffed. *Informs:* how many agents, in what shift shape, are actually needed. We have no absolute volume in hand; this is the number everything hangs on. Rough ceiling: 12 agents × ~8 productive hours ≈ 96 agent-hours/day ≈ ~1,000 calls/day at 5.6-min AHT (assuming ~8 productive hours/agent) — if weekly demand is well above ~4,500 calls, staffing tweaks alone can't get under 2 minutes.
2. **AHT decomposition by call type and agent, before vs. after March.** *Informs:* how much of the 4.1→5.6 regression is training/workflow (recoverable) vs. structural — i.e., whether AHT recovery can substitute for headcount.
3. **Repeat-caller analysis:** what the 18% multi-callers called about, and whether their first contact ended in a transfer or an unkept promise. *Informs:* how much demand is self-inflicted (including abandon-and-redial) and the size of the first-contact-resolution prize.
4. **True demand including abandoned calls**, and whether March volume rose alongside AHT. *Informs:* the real baseline — 21% abandonment means answered-call stats understate load, and hiring math built on answered calls will be wrong.
5. **Clinic refill turnaround time** and the share of refill-status calls that need no agent action ("just checking"). *Informs:* how much of the 31% can be deflected via notifications/IVR vs. requires clinic workflow change.
6. **Portal capability audit:** can the portal actually book appointments, request refills, and release results — and where is activation lowest? *Informs:* whether self-service deflection (45% appointment calls + 31% refill calls = 76% of volume in three self-serviceable categories) can land this quarter or is a next-quarter play.

## What would kill the idea

- **Kill (a) hire 4 agents if:** the load analysis shows utilization is acceptable outside Monday 8:00–11:00 — then the problem is a ~3× peak, not a weekly shortfall, and FTE bought for Monday sits idle Tuesday–Friday (staggered/part-time peak coverage is cheaper). Also kill it if AHT recovery (back toward ~4.5 min) plus repeat-call reduction closes the gap — capacity was misdiagnosed. And if true demand exceeds even 16 agents' capacity, 4 hires won't fix it and the money belongs in demand reduction.
- **Kill (b) callback if:** it can't deploy this quarter or integrates poorly (agents lack context for callbacks). Also kill it if peak-hour utilization is at or above 100% — callbacks then just convert abandonment into a backlog and can add work (failed contact attempts), cosmetically improving the metric while patients still wait. I won't sign up for that.
- **Kill my leading idea (AHT recovery + demand deflection) if:** refill-status calls turn out to be mostly complex clinical questions rather than status checks, and clinic turnaround can't change; if the portal can't do booking/refills/results and the roadmap is months out; or if the AHT jump is structural (mandatory EHR steps, not fixable by training). Each shrinks a lever and shifts the answer back toward staffing.

## Top 3 priorities

1. **Recover the AHT regression and cut transfer double-handling.** AHT +37% since March while capacity fell ~14% (2 vacancies) is the smoking gun, and queue wait explodes nonlinearly as utilization approaches 100%. Training, workflow fixes, and menu/routing repair are cheap, fast, and multiply every other lever.
2. **Deflect the refill-status wave at the source.** 31% of calls, plus a likely share of repeat callers: proactive status texts/portal notifications, IVR self-service lookup, and clinic turnaround fixes. This is the biggest single demand reduction available, and it pairs with pushing portal activation (35% today) at check-in across all 40 clinics.
3. **Reshape capacity to the demand curve, not a flat block.** Backfill the 2 vacancies now; use the load data to add targeted Monday-morning coverage (22% of weekly calls in 3 hours ≈ 3× the average hourly rate, assuming calls are otherwise spread evenly) via staggered shifts or part-timers rather than 4 uniform FTE; run callback as the stopgap. A flat 8:00–17:00 block can never fix a 3× peak.
