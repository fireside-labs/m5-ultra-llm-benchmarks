## Variables

*No financial or demographic figures were provided, so every item below is framed as a check rather than a conclusion. I assume a ~10-minute drive-time catchment for primary care (walkable for Site B) and ~15 minutes for urgent care.*

1. **What the clinic is actually for.** Growth, safety-net access, and cheap consolidation each favour a different site, so every other variable's weight is undefined until the board fixes the primary success metric. *Check:* make the board rank net-new patients, Medicaid lives served, and contribution margin before any site scoring happens.
2. **Catchment demand and demographics.** Volume comes from residents within the catchment, and "fastest-growing" (A) often means young, healthy households with fewer visits per capita, while a dense older area (B) carries more chronic disease and more visits. *Check:* drive-time isochrones, census projections, age profile, and visits-per-capita benchmarks.
3. **Payer mix in the catchment.** Revenue per visit, not visit count, pays the rent — B's uninsured/Medicaid base means low reimbursement and charity-care drag, while A likely skews commercial. *Check:* ZIP-level payer distribution from claims/ESRI data plus Harlow's own Medicaid denial and self-pay write-off history.
4. **Net-new vs. cannibalised volume.** C sits 1 mile from an existing Harlow clinic, so it would largely re-home current patients, and the competitor urgent care across the street both validates and splits walk-in demand. *Check:* patient-origin analysis of the existing clinic by ZIP, plus competitor foot-traffic data or posted wait times.
5. **Competitive supply already in each catchment.** Fast-growth corridors attract every urgent-care chain and primary-care group, and if an FQHC already covers B, the marginal access gain there shrinks. *Check:* provider density per 10,000 residents, permit-filed or announced projects near A, and FQHC locations relative to B.
6. **Staffing feasibility.** Clinics fail on recruitment more often than on rent; C's hospital proximity eases shared staffing and urgent-care transfers — though neither actually requires co-location. *Check:* a recruitment market scan per location, and whether B's area is a designated HPSA/MUA, which unlocks loan-repayment recruiting.
7. **Total cost of occupancy, not headline rent.** A's lowest rent per square foot ignores medical fit-out (plumbing, X-ray shielding, HVAC), and B's "major renovation" is the wildcard. *Check:* a 10-year pro forma per site including tenant improvements, plus a property-condition assessment and Phase I/II environmental on the bank building.
8. **Time to open.** C is ready now, A waits on the retail centre's delivery, and B waits on renovation with overrun risk — delay is forgone revenue and forgone mission. *Check:* permitting plus build schedule to first patient for each site.
9. **Transportation fit to the actual population.** B's two bus lines may be the only realistic access for a carless Medicaid catchment, while A's ample parking fits a car-dependent suburb. *Check:* carless-household share and transit ridership around B; drive-time coverage for A and C.
10. **Zoning and technical fit for the service mix.** X-ray (shielding plus state machine registration), a lab draw station, urgent-care patient flow, and change-of-use approvals vary by building and zone. *Check:* zoning confirmation and a test space plan per site before the board votes.
11. **Urgent-care siting physics.** Walk-in demand peaks evenings and weekends and follows visibility and convenience — retail co-tenancy (A) suits it; an office park (C) typically empties at 6pm. *Check:* traffic counts, co-tenancy mix, and evening/weekend population around each site.
12. **Mission and community-benefit alignment.** As a non-profit, siting in the underserved area supports CHNA priorities and the community-benefit narrative that underpins the tax exemption. *Check:* map each site against the current CHNA priority areas.

## Top 3 priorities

1. **The clinic's intended role (variable 1).** Each site is "best" for a different objective — A for net-new commercial growth, B for safety-net access, C for cheap and fast consolidation — so until the board names the primary metric, any site ranking is arbitrary. This is the crux, and it costs one meeting to resolve.
2. **Net-new demand × payer mix (variables 2–3).** Incremental patients times payment per visit is the entire economic engine; note that population growth (A) is not the same as visit demand, and visit volume (B) is not the same as revenue.
3. **Cannibalisation and competitive overlap (variables 4–5).** The decision must be made on incremental volume, not gross: C's proximity to your own clinic and to a competitor makes it the likely worst on this test even though it looks "safest" on cost and speed.

## What would change my mind

- **The board defines the objective differently.** If safety-net access is the stated goal, B becomes the favourite despite the payer mix; if near-term margin is the goal, A; if minimal capital risk is the goal, C — which is exactly why priority 1 comes first.
- **Patient-origin data on Site C.** If the ZIPs around C already send most volume to Harlow's existing clinic, that confirms C adds roughly zero net-new patients; but if those ZIPs show heavy leakage to competitors, C becomes a recapture play and jumps the ranking.
- **Site B's property-condition assessment.** If renovation exceeds roughly 12–18 months or costs approach new-build (my assumption — no budget figures were given), B drops out; conversely, HPSA/MUA designation plus grant funding could meaningfully offset its weak payer mix.
- **Committed competitor entries in A's corridor.** Filed permits or signed leases for urgent care or primary-care groups near A would erode its net-new advantage and turn "fastest-growing" into "most contested."
- **A mystery-shopper audit showing short new-patient wait times metro-wide.** That would weaken the premise of the whole exercise — the cheapest "site" may be none of the three, and the board should first prove an access gap exists before spending capital.
