    **Case for (Remote-by-default):**
    - Talent pool and retention: Access to national/global talent, reduces attrition from life events (relocation, family).
    - Focus and deep work: Knowledge work (software, finance, ops) requires deep concentration; office interruptions and commute time are pure waste. Async communication forces better documentation.
    - Autonomy and trust: Signals trust, increases intrinsic motivation, reduces surveillance overhead. Teams decide when to meet in person (hybrid flexibility) based on actual need, not calendar.
    - Cost and speed: Less real estate, less overhead, faster hiring, less time-to-productivity (no onboarding to a specific desk culture).
    - Resilience and continuity: No single point of failure (weather, pandemics, local disruptions). Operations and software can run 24/7 across time zones.
    - Reduced coordination overhead: Forcing 3 days in office creates a "phantom" presence where people are physically there but on video calls with remote colleagues. Default remote makes the medium explicit.

    **Case against (keep 3 days office / not default remote):**
    - Serendipity and tacit knowledge: Innovation and problem-solving often come from unplanned encounters, hallway conversations, and reading non-verbal cues. Finance and ops require cross-functional alignment that is harder to build async.
    - Mentorship and onboarding: Junior staff learn by observing senior colleagues, hearing context, and being pulled into conversations. Remote makes this slower and more deliberate, risking a generation gap.
    - Culture and belonging: Shared identity, norms, and trust are built through shared physical presence. Remote-by-default risks fragmentation into silos, lower engagement, and higher turnover for those who value social connection.
    - Coordination cost for complex tasks: Software and finance/ops involve tight interdependencies, code reviews, sprint planning, and crisis management. Async communication can slow down urgent, high-stakes decisions (e.g., financial close, outage).
    - Managerial capability and fairness: Not all managers are good at remote management. A default remote policy could widen performance gaps, and teams that choose to meet in person might create a two-tier system (in-group vs out-group).
    - Innovation cycles: The "default" matters. If remote is default, the friction to meet in person is high, so teams will default to remote even when in-person would be much better. The current 3-day rule forces a baseline of interaction.
