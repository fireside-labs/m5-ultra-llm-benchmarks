## Recommendation
Choose the phased mix: 6 electric vans plus 6 diesel vans now. Eight-year fleet cost is ~$1,494,000 (6 × $118,000 + 6 × $131,000), versus $1,572,000 for all-diesel and $1,656,000 for all-electric (12 × $118,000 + the $240,000 utility upgrade the TCO table omits). It is the cheapest option on the numbers given, and it fits the depot's existing electrical service exactly.

## Key reasons
- **Lowest total cost:** saves ~$78,000 vs all-diesel and ~$162,000 vs all-electric. The electric van's $13,000/van advantage is real but small; spreading the $240,000 upgrade over 12 vans ($20,000/van) more than erases it.
- **Infrastructure fit:** 6 chargers sit within the current service limit, and the electric purchase price already includes its charger.
- **Operations fit:** the longest route (160 km) uses ~70% of the 230 km winter range, with overnight depot charging, so range is not a constraint.
- **Warranty covers the horizon:** at 30,000 km/yr, each electric van reaches ~240,000 km in 8 years, just under the 250,000 km battery warranty cap.
- **Hedge value:** if electrics prove unreliable, only half the fleet is exposed, and the diesel half's replacement is a natural decision point with better operating data.

## Risks
- **Thin warranty margin:** only ~1,250 km/yr of headroom; route growth past ~31,250 km/yr voids coverage before year 8, and an out-of-warranty battery cost is not in the TCO.
- **Deferred, not avoided, cost:** going fully electric later still triggers the $240,000 upgrade.
- **Assumption risk:** resale values and the diesel/electricity price gap could shift; a diesel price fall narrows the $13,000/van advantage.
- **No charger redundancy:** exactly 6 chargers means a charger failure strands an electric van's overnight charge.
