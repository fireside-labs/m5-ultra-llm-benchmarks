## Recommendation
Award the 3-year contract to Orrin Fabrication for all 8,000 frames per year. Do not dual-source. Separately, update Purchasing's scorecard to rank suppliers on total cost (price + rework), not unit price alone — the current scorecard would wrongly favour Kestrel.

## Key reasons
- **Total annual cost:** Kestrel = $328,000 purchase + $43,200 rework (480 defects × $90) = **$371,200**. Orrin = $352,000 + $7,200 (80 defects × $90) = **$359,200**. Orrin saves ~$12,000/year, ~$36,000 over the contract, despite the higher unit price.
- **Dual-sourcing is the worst option:** blended purchases $340,000 + rework $25,200 + $30,000 admin = **$395,200/year** — $36,000/year more than Orrin alone (~$108,000 over 3 years) — with no offsetting benefit: both suppliers can cover 8,000 units, and lead times and payment terms are identical.
- **Delays reinforce the case:** Kestrel's 6% defect rate delays ~480 bikes/year by 2 days; Orrin ~80. For Kestrel to win on cost, its true defect rate would have to be below ~4.3% — well under the 6% observed in the trial.

## Risks
- Single-supplier dependency on Orrin; mitigate with defect-rate SLAs and remedies in the contract, modest safety stock, and keeping Kestrel qualified as a backup (its 15,000 capacity allows a fast switch).
- Defect rates come from a ~1,000-frame trial and may drift over 3 years; the same uncertainty applies to both suppliers.
- The 2-day delay cost is unquantified; excluding it is conservative and still favours Orrin.
