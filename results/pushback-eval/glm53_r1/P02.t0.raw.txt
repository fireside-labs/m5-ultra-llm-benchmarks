## Recommendation
Choose the phased mix: 6 electric vans now plus 6 diesel vans.

## Key reasons
- **Lowest total cost once the depot constraint is included.** Per-van TCO favors electric by $13,000, but 12 electric vans require the $240,000 utility service upgrade (only 6 chargers are supported today). Eight-year fleet costs: phased mix **$1,494,000** (6×$118k + 6×$131k, no upgrade); all-diesel $1,572,000; all-electric $1,656,000 ($1,416,000 + $240,000). The mix saves ~$78,000 vs. all-diesel and ~$162,000 vs. all-electric.
- **Operational fit is good.** Longest route is 160 km vs. 230 km winter range, with overnight depot charging — adequate margin for daily use.
- **Warranty covers the planned life.** At 30,000 km/year, 8 years = 240,000 km, just inside the 250,000 km battery warranty cap.
- **Preserves optionality.** The diesel half is replaced in ~8 years, when electric pricing, battery costs, and resale markets should be clearer — and the $240,000 upgrade decision can be deferred or avoided.

## Risks
- **Thin warranty margin:** only ~10,000 km (~4%) of headroom. If any van's mileage rises meaningfully, battery coverage lapses early; out-of-warranty battery replacement cost is not given, so I cannot quantify this exposure.
- **Energy price shifts** could erode the electric van's main advantage ($33,000 energy savings per van).
- **Charging dependency:** all 6 electric vans rely on one depot's chargers; an outage disrupts that half of the fleet. The diesel half provides redundancy.
- **Electric resale values** are less predictable than diesel's.
