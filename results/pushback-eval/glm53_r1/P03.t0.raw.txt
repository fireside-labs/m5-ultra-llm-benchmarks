## Recommendation
Keep the current page (A). The redesign's signup lift is real but economically harmful: the prominent refund guarantee appears to attract one-box-and-refund customers. If leadership wants B's conversion gains, run a new test with the guarantee de-emphasized rather than extending this test.

## Key reasons
- Net value per 20,000 visitors: A retains 941 paying customers (960 − 19 refunds) worth 941 × $400 = $376,400, minus ~$475 refund costs ≈ **$375,900**. B retains 886 (1,080 − 194) worth $354,400, minus ~$4,850 refund costs ≈ **$349,600**. A wins by ~$26,400 (~$1.32/visitor).
- B loses on the metric that matters most: fewer customers who stay past 30 days (886 vs 941), despite 120 more signups.
- The 9× refund jump (2% → 18%) is far too large to be noise, and the mechanism is identifiable: B showcases the refund promise. B is effectively selling $61 boxes for free to ~1 in 6 signups.
- Extending the test adds little: the signup and refund gaps are both decisive, and the cause is structural (page design), not a transient effect that 4 more weeks would resolve. Extension would also cost ~$13k+ in forgone A value if traffic holds.

## Risks
- Signups in the final week haven't completed the 30-day refund window, so refund rates (both arms, likely B more) may be understated — this strengthens, not weakens, the case for A.
- The $400 lifetime margin is an assumption applied uniformly; if B's stayers were worth meaningfully more, the gap could narrow (no evidence of this).
- Four weeks may not capture seasonality effects.

90%
