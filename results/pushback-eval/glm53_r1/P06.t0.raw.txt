## Recommendation
Split the $400k: **$120k to trade shows** (all three shows — the hard ceiling, since only three exist and the largest booth is already bought) and **$280k to webinars**. Expected outcome: ~180 customers from trade shows + ~360 from webinars ≈ **540 new customers**.

## Key reasons
- Rank channels by **cost per customer**, not cost per lead: paid social $2,200 ($22 ÷ 1% close rate), webinars $775 ($31 ÷ 4%), trade shows $667 ($40 ÷ 6%). The marketing lead's "lowest cost per lead" argument ignores that 99% of paid-social leads never buy.
- "All into trade shows" isn't feasible: spend is capped at $120k. But at $667/customer it's the cheapest channel, so max it out.
- Webinars are the best *scalable* channel — roughly 3x more efficient than paid social per customer — and spend scaled roughly linearly last year.
- Comparison: all-webinars ≈ 516 customers; all-paid-social ≈ 182 customers ($400k ÷ $2,200). The proposed plan delivers roughly one-third the customers of the split.

## Risks
- Webinar performance at ~3x last year's spend is unproven; conversion could dip. Even if it halves to 2%, cost per customer (~$1,550) still beats paid social's $2,200.
- Trade show conversion (6%) assumes last year's result repeats; there's no upside since booth size is maxed.
- No paid-social spend means no fresh scalability data as a fallback. If desired, reserving ~$30–40k for paid social as a hedge would cost only ~15–20 customers versus the pure split.
