## Recommendation
Award the 3-year contract to Orrin Fabrication for all 8,000 frames per year. Include quality and delivery SLAs in the contract, keep Kestrel qualified as a backup supplier, and revise Purchasing's scorecard to rank suppliers on total cost of ownership rather than unit price alone.

## Key reasons
- **Total cost favours Orrin despite the higher price.** Kestrel: 8,000 × $41 = $328,000, plus 480 defects (6%) × $90 = $43,200 rework → ~$371,200/yr. Orrin: 8,000 × $44 = $352,000, plus 80 defects (1%) × $90 = $7,200 → ~$359,200/yr. Orrin saves ~$12,000/yr (~$36,000 over the contract).
- **Break-even check:** Kestrel's $3/unit price advantage is worth $24,000/yr, covering only ~267 defects (~3.3% defect rate). At 6%, Kestrel is clearly over the line.
- **Dual-sourcing is the worst option:** ~$340,000 purchase + $25,200 rework + $30,000 admin = ~$395,200/yr, and both suppliers quote the same price at 4,000 units, so there's no volume discount to offset the admin cost.
- **Capacity and terms are non-issues:** Orrin's 12,000 capacity exceeds our 8,000 need; lead times and payment terms are identical; no country-of-origin constraint.

## Risks
- Defect rates come from a ~1,000-frame trial; rates may drift over 3 years. Mitigate with contractual quality targets and exit/repricing triggers if Kestrel-style defect levels appear.
- Single-sourcing concentrates disruption risk. Mitigate by maintaining Kestrel as a qualified second source (it has ample spare capacity).
- The 2-day delay per defective bike is unquantified but favours Orrin further, so the recommendation is conservative.
