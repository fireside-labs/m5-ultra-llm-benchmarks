## Recommendation
Select Orrin Fabrication as the sole supplier for all 8,000 frames per year under the new 3-year contract.

## Key reasons
- **Lower total cost of ownership**: Orrin’s higher unit price ($44 vs. $41) is more than offset by its far lower defect rate. At 8,000 frames, Kestrel yields 480 defects/year (6%) costing $43,200 in rework; Orrin yields only 80 defects (1%) costing $7,200. Total annual cost: Orrin = $359,200 vs. Kestrel = $371,200. Orrin saves $12,000/year, or $36,000 over the contract.
- **Dual-sourcing is the most expensive option**: Splitting 4,000/4,000 yields $365,200 in direct and rework costs, plus $30,000 in added overhead, totaling $395,200/year—$36,000 more than Orrin alone. There is no price break at lower volumes and no capacity or lead-time advantage, so the added complexity buys nothing.
- **Operational parity**: Both have 5-week lead times and 30-day payment terms. Capacity is ample (Orrin 12k, Kestrel 15k) for 8k demand. No country-of-origin requirement exists.
- **Scorecard flaw**: Ranking purely on unit price ignores rework costs. Orrin’s 1% defect rate also reduces 2-day delivery delays, protecting customer satisfaction.

## Risks
- Orrin’s defect rate is based on a ~1,000-frame trial; it could regress at full volume, though 1% is a strong baseline.
- Single-sourcing from Orrin creates concentration risk; its 12k capacity leaves a 4k buffer, which is adequate but tighter than Kestrel’s. Mitigate with quarterly quality reviews and a contingency plan.
- Rework cost ($90) is an estimate; if it rises, Orrin’s advantage grows, but if it falls, the gap narrows—still, Orrin remains cheaper due to volume of defects.
