## Recommendation
Do not put the whole budget into paid social. Instead, allocate **$120,000 to trade shows** (the maximum possible, covering all three shows) and the remaining **$280,000 to webinars**. This split yields an estimated **541 new customers** (180 from trade shows + ~361 from webinars), versus 516 for all-webinars and only 181 for all-paid-social.

## Key reasons
- The true metric is **cost per acquired customer (CAC)**, not cost per lead. Paid social CAC is $2,200 ($22 / 1% conversion), webinars is $775 ($31 / 4%), and trade shows is $667 ($40 / 6%). Paid social is the worst channel by far.
- Trade shows have the lowest CAC, but they are **capped at $120,000** (3 shows, largest booth already purchased). You cannot spend more there effectively.
- Since webinars and paid social scale linearly, and webinars have a significantly lower CAC, webinars strictly dominate paid social. There is no scenario where paid social is the optimal marginal spend.
- The optimal strategy is to exhaust the cheapest channel (trade shows) up to its physical limit, then invest the next-best scalable channel (webinars) with the remainder.

## Risks
- The linear scaling assumption may break down at higher volumes—webinar lead quality could degrade if spend triples, or conversion rates might shift. However, even if webinar conversion drops by half (to 2%), the split still yields ~360 customers, far better than paid social (181) and more robust than all-webinars.
- Trade show leads may have longer sales cycles, but the 6-month conversion window is assumed constant across channels.
