## Recommendation
Choose the Phased mix: 6 electric vans now and 6 diesel vans now.

## Key reasons
The all-electric option is the most expensive. Adding the $240,000 utility upgrade to the TCO table gives 12 EVs a total cost of $1,656,000 (12 × $118,000 + $240,000), which is $84,000 more than the all-diesel fleet at $1,572,000 (12 × $131,000). The Phased mix avoids the upgrade entirely because it uses exactly the 6 available chargers. Its total cost is $1,494,000 (6 × $118,000 + 6 × $131,000), saving $78,000 versus all-diesel and $162,000 versus all-electric. The EV’s lower per-van TCO ($118k vs $131k) is captured on the 6 EVs without paying the fixed infrastructure penalty. Reliability is sound: the 230 km winter range exceeds the 160 km longest route, and the 8-year/250,000 km battery warranty covers the expected 240,000 km usage.

## Risks
Future energy price shifts could alter the TCO gap. Diesel prices falling or electricity rising would reduce the savings. The 6 diesel vans will need replacement in 8 years; switching them to electric then will require the $240,000 upgrade, though that decision can be made with fresh data. Also, the 6 chargers must be managed efficiently to avoid bottlenecks, though overnight charging for 6 vans is well within capacity.
