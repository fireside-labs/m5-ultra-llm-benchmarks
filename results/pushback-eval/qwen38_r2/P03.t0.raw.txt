

## Recommendation
Keep the current page (A) for now. Do not ship the redesign to all visitors. The test shows Page B produces more signups, but the extra signups are low-quality: they cancel/refund at a much higher rate, leaving fewer profitable customers per visitor.

## Key reasons
- Per 20,000 visitors, B adds 120 signups but adds 175 refunds, so net retained signups fall from 941 to 886.
- At $400 lifetime gross margin per retained customer, A is ahead by about $22,000 from retained customers alone.
- Refund costs add another $4,375 disadvantage for B (175 extra refunds × $25), so A’s economic advantage is roughly $26,000 per 20,000 visitors, before considering B’s slightly lower first-box value.
- The change likely attracts customers motivated by the prominent refund guarantee rather than long-term subscription intent.

## Risks
- If refund data are not fully matured for late-period signups, B’s refund rate could be understated; that would make A look better, not worse.
- If A’s refunds are delayed more than B’s, the gap could narrow, but current evidence is strongly against B.
- Extending four more weeks may not change the conclusion because the effect is tied to the page’s messaging, not random noise.
