

## Recommendation
Award the 3-year contract to Orrin Fabrication for all 8,000 frames, with Kestrel Metalworks retained as a qualified backup rather than a primary dual source.

## Key reasons
Orrin has much lower quality risk. At 8,000 frames, expected rework cost is $7,200/year for Orrin (1% defects × $90) versus $43,200/year for Kestrel (6% defects × $90). Orrin’s $3/unit price premium costs $24,000/year, but this is more than offset by $36,000 lower rework, making Orrin about $12,000/year cheaper before counting 2-day rework delays.

Dual-sourcing is worse: purchase cost is $340,000, expected rework is about $25,200, plus $30,000 admin/audit/logistics, totaling about $395,200/year. Orrin’s 12,000-frame capacity covers the 8,000-frame requirement, and both suppliers have the same 5-week lead time and 30-day payment terms. Kestrel’s lower unit price is misleading because the current scorecard ignores defect-driven costs and delays.

## Risks
The defect estimates come from only about 1,000 frames per supplier, so rates could vary; Orrin’s 1% could worsen, or Kestrel’s 6% could improve. Single-sourcing Orrin creates supply-disruption risk, though Kestrel has enough capacity to backstop. Orrin’s capacity headroom is 4,000 frames, less than Kestrel’s 7,000-frame headroom. Incoming defect rates should be monitored and contractual quality penalties or rework cost recovery should be included.
