# The System Says You're Fine

## Chapter 1: Vital Signs

I died on a Tuesday, holding a stranger's heart in my hands.

Not metaphorically. Not in the poetic, weeping-at-the-autopsy-report way. I was elbow-deep in the thoracic cavity of a nineteen-year-old motorcycle courier, cutting a suture around a lacerated left ventricle while the anesthesiologist called out pressures and the nurses kept the field clear and the whole thing was going *fine*, going *right*, the way it goes when you've done four hundred of these and your hands remember what your exhausted brain can't.

Then the OR lights flickered. The backup generator kicked in with a sound like a dying animal. And I felt the small, specific *pop* in my chest that I would later, in retrospect, identify as my own aortic arch giving up the ghost.

I didn't feel pain. I felt *separation*. Like a radio losing its station mid-song.

The last thing I saw was the courier's monitor, still beeping steadily, still saying *I'm fine, I'm fine, I'm fine*, while my own heart became a slow, wet, irrelevant drum beneath my ribs.

Then there was a corridor of white light, which I want to note was *terrible* lighting design, and a voice that said, with the enthusiasm of a middle-management email,

*Welcome, Doctor. The Kingdom of Aethermere requires a healer-class adventurer. Please press your face against the following prompt to confirm.*

I did not press my face against the prompt.

I blacked out.

---

I woke up face-down in dirt that smelled like iron and old sheep.

My first thought was *I'm still in the OR.* My second thought was *the floor of the OR does not smell like old sheep.* My third thought was that I was breathing, which was interesting, because I was fairly certain that my aortic arch had finished its final monologue.

I pushed myself up. My hands were younger. Smooth. Unscarred, which was wrong—I had a scar across my left palm from a shattered vial in residency, 2019. The scar was gone. My hands were the hands of a twenty-two-year-old, which I had not been in eleven years.

I was wearing a linen tunic and leather bracers. Around my neck hung a pendant shaped like a caduceus, except the snakes were replaced with tiny interlocking gears.

And floating in my peripheral vision, translucent and faintly blue, was a rectangle of text.

> **STATUS**
> **Name:** Kael Oris
> **Class:** Healer (Novice, Lv. 1)
> **HP:** 340/340
> **MP:** 120/120
> **STR:** 8 | **DEX:** 9 | **CON:** 11 | **INT:** 22 | **WIS:** 24 | **CHA:** 13
> **Skills:** Minor Mend (Lv.1), Purify (Lv.1), Appraisal (Lv.1)
> **Gold:** 0
> **Active Quests:** None
> **Party:** None

I stared at it. It stared back, gently pulsing.

"Okay," I said, to no one, in a voice that wasn't quite my voice but was close enough. "Okay. I'm dead. I've been *assigned* a job. In a fantasy world. With a *spreadsheet*."

The status screen did not respond. It simply existed, the way a persistent pop-up ad exists, the way a medical chart exists, the way the whole goddamn system of biology exists: an interface imposed on a messy, bleeding reality to make it *legible to people who don't want to think about it.*

I understood interfaces. I understood what they left out.

---

I learned the rest in fragments, from the panicked woman shaking my shoulder and the two men arguing ten feet away.

I'd been *summoned*, apparently. The Healer's Accord in the city of Velmara had a contractual obligation to field a healer for every registered adventuring party, and they'd used a divination circle to pull a "compatible soul" from "elsewhere." The woman shaking my shoulder was Drenna, a Level 3 Scout with a rusted shortbow and a squint that suggested she'd been peering into the middle distance her entire life. The two men were Brask and Callum, Fighters, Levels 4 and 3 respectively, both missing teeth, both deeply and vocally unhappy that their new healer looked like a first-year medical student who'd been yanked from a nap.

"She's got INT of twenty-two," Callum said, reading my status screen over my shoulder. "That's good. That's actually real good. The last healer we had had an INT of fourteen and couldn't tell a poison wound from a splinter."

"She's *Level 1*," Brask said. "We have a *dungeon crawl* in two hours, Callum. The Gristle Hollow. We need a healer who can keep *Minor Mend* up through three combat waves without going to zero MP."

"I can read the *system*, Brask, I can't read *her*."

I stood up, brushed the dirt off my tunic, and looked at the three of them. Something was asserting itself in my chest—not courage, exactly. More like the specific, exhausted calm of showing up to a fourteen-hour shift at 0600 and saying *alright, who's trying to die today.*

"Let's do this," I said. "Walk me through what I'm supposed to do. And don't show me the skill descriptions. Show me what's supposed to *happen to the body.*"

They looked at me like I'd suggested eating the dungeon boss raw.

But Drenna, at least, said, "Alright, doc. Let me explain how the Gristle Hollow works."

---

The Gristle Hollow was a cave system three miles east of Velmara, populated by a species of oversized, chitinous beetles that the locals called gristles. Drenna walked us up to the entrance while explaining the dungeon's layout—three waves of gristles, each wave bigger than the last, capped by an "elite" specimen the system labeled **Gristle Matriarch, Lv. 7.**

"The way healing works," Drenna said, as we crouched in the mud outside the cave mouth, "is that you cast Minor Mend, and the target's HP goes up. Simple. The system handles the body. You just manage the number."

"And if someone gets hit in a specific spot?" I asked. "A joint. An artery. A—"

"The system handles it," Drenna said again, with the patient finality of a nurse telling a family member that the CT scan was *probably* nothing. "HP is HP. You don't need to worry about the anatomy. That's what the system is *for.*"

I nodded. I filed it under *things I will absolutely be worrying about.*

We went in.

---

The first two waves were, I would admit, almost manageable. The gristles were the size of large dogs and fought like large dogs—lunge, bite, back off. Brask tanked the front line with a round shield and a cursed mace that dealt bonus damage to "unclean" creatures. Callum flanked. Drenna put arrows in legs and joints. And I stood in the back, watching the floating HP bars above everyone's heads, and cast Minor Mend whenever a bar dipped below two-thirds.

It worked. It *worked.* The system registered a slash across Brask's forearm, subtracted 45 HP, and when I touched his arm and said the activation word, the HP went up, the wound closed, the pain faded. Brask flexed his fingers and grinned.

"See?" he said. "Told you twenty-two INT was good. You barely have to *think.*"

I smiled at him. I did not tell him that when I'd touched his arm, I'd felt the wound close and it had closed *wrong*, the muscle fibers knitting in a haphazard pattern that would keep him from fully supinating his wrist for the rest of his life, and that the system didn't care, because his HP bar was full, and the system said he was *fine.*

I did not tell him because I wasn't sure how to, yet.

The third wave was where it went sideways.

The gristles came fast, more of them, and Callum took a hit on the neck—a gristle's mandible punched through his collar and *bored in*, deep, just below the ear. His HP bar dropped from 412 to 290. The system logged it as **Wound: Piercing (Moderate).**

"Minor Mend!" Callum barked, already reaching for me.

I reached for him. I cast. His HP climbed back to 412. The wound closed. He touched his neck, shrugged, said "See? Fine."

But I'd looked. I'd *looked.* And beneath the closed skin, I could see—could *feel*, through some new sensitivity that twenty-two INT and twenty-four WIS had apparently granted me—a pooling of dark blood behind the sternocleidomastoid. A hematoma forming. The mandible had nicked the internal jugular, and the system had *sealed the entry wound* without addressing the vessel beneath it.

Callum was bleeding into his own neck. Slowly. Invisibly. The system would not register it. The system had *already given him full HP.*

He would collapse in perhaps ten minutes. His airway would occlude. And the system would log it as **HP: 0. Cause of death: trauma.** And that would be the end of it. Because the system said he was fine.

The system said you're *fine.*

"Nobody move," I said.

I grabbed Callum's shoulders. I grabbed Drenna's shoulder with my other hand. "Brask, Callum—*down.* Drenna, give me your knife. The clean one. The one you use for—"

"Doc, his HP is *full.*"

"I *know* his HP is full."

I didn't know if I could do what I was about to try. There was no skill for it. No activation word. No blue rectangle in my peripheral vision offering a menu of options. The system had no button for *open the neck and clamp the vessel with your fingers.*

But I had hands. I had eleven years of doing exactly this. And I had a three-inch blade and a man who was, in the system's loving, idiotic, *benevolent* judgment, perfectly *fine.*

I made the cut.

Drenna screamed. Brask shouted. Callum went rigid and then went *very* still as his own blood welled up around my fingers and I reached in, past the system-sealed skin, past the system's neat little number, and found the vessel, and *pinned it*, and watched the dark red stop pooling.

And then, just as I clamped my fingers shut and breathed, a new notification pinged in the corner of my vision. Small. Red. Text I had not seen before.

> **SYSTEM NOTICE:**
> **Unregistered procedure detected. Anatomical intervention outside system parameters.**
> **This action has no corresponding skill entry.**
> **This action was not authorized by the Healer's Accord spell framework.**
> **Flagged for review.**
>
> **Query: How does the subject know this?**

I looked up from Callum's open neck, blood to my wrists, and met the empty air where the text hung, pulsing.

*How does the subject know this?*

I'd been doing this for eleven years. Before I'd been Kael Oris, Level 1 Healer. Before the corridor of white light and the middle-management voice. Before I'd been a *number in a spreadsheet.*

I'd been me.

And the system, for the first time in whatever long, ancient, *designed* existence it had, had noticed.

"Get me thread," I said to Drenna, still pinching the vein. "And don't look at the blue boxes. Look at *him.*"

She looked at him. His face was grey. His HP said full.

She looked at the grey, and she brought me the thread.

Outside the cave, the sun was going down. Somewhere in Velmara, a divination circle was cooling. Somewhere in the architecture of this world, in the deep roots beneath the blue rectangles and the floating numbers, something *shifted.*

Something that had been waiting a very long time for someone to stop looking at the screen and start looking at the *patient.*

I tied off the vessel. I closed the wound with my hands, with thread, with the slow, imperfect, *real* work of mending flesh. Callum's HP bar didn't flicker. The system never saw it.

But when I was done, and my fingers were shaking, and Brask was asking me if I was *crazy*, and Drenna was handing me a canteen with hands that wouldn't stop trembling—

A new skill appeared in my peripheral vision. Small. Unnamed. No description. No level. Just a single line of text that seemed to be *looking at me* as much as I was looking at it.

> **[???] — (Lv. 1) — Unlearned. Unlearnable. The old doctor's way.**

I stared at it.

It stared back.

And somewhere far below us, in the dark of the Gristle Hollow, the Matriarch—Level 7, a *dungeon boss*, a *system-fixed encounter*—turned in her tunnel and *listened* to the sound of a healer doing something the system said was impossible.

Chapter 1 end.
