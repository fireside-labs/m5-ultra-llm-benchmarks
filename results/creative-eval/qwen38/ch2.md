# The System Says You're Fine

## Chapter 2: Differential Diagnosis

Callum threw up in the dark of the cave.

Not dramatically. Not the cinematic, heaving, *I've seen too much* kind of vomit. A short, wet, embarrassed little sound, and then Drenna turned away with the practiced patience of a woman who had scouted twenty-three dungeons and found that the scariest thing in each one was usually the taste of the adventurer's last meal making a re-appearance.

"Sorry," Callum whispered. He touched his neck. The skin was closed. My thread-work was, if I was being honest, ugly. The system would have made it seamless. The system made everything seamless, which was the whole problem. You couldn't *feel* the seams, so you never checked them. "Doc. *Doc.* My HP is—"

"I know what your HP is, Callum. I can see it. It's four hundred and twelve. It has been four hundred and twelve for the last six minutes. What your HP *is not* telling you is that you were about three minutes from aspirating your own hematoma into your trachea."

Silence. The kind of silence that has a texture. Wet and cold and full of beetles clicking their mandibles somewhere deeper in the dark.

Brask said, very quietly, "The system sealed the wound."

"Yes."

"The system said he was *fine.*"

"Yes."

"And you cut him open *anyway.*"

"Yes."

Brask stared at me. I stared back. His hand rested on the pommel of his cursed mace, and I could see him running the math—four levels above me, a guy who'd been doing this since before the Healer's Accord started pulling confused strangers out of the sky, a guy who had *never* once questioned whether the blue rectangle in his peripheral vision was lying.

"You're heretical," he said. Not angry. Almost *wondering.* Like a man watching someone walk through a wall and trying to decide whether to call a priest or take notes.

"I'm a doctor," I said. I wiped my hands on my tunic. I didn't have gauze. I didn't have gloves. I didn't have a scalpel, or a suction line, or a second set of hands. What I had was a rusted scouting knife, a length of thread Drenna had pulled from her own pack, and the small, terrifying knowledge that the thing I'd just done was *real* in a way that nothing else in this world seemed to be.

The [???] skill pulsed at the edge of my vision. I'd been trying to ignore it. I was failing.

"Right," Drenna said, from the cave mouth. She hadn't turned around. When she did, her squint was gone, replaced by something flat and sharp. "Right. Can you do that again? For the Matriarch?"

"I can't cut open a Level 7 dungeon boss, Drenna."

"You can't *heal* one either. You're Level 1. Minor Mend does forty HP a tick. The Matriarch has four thousand. Brask can chip maybe thirty a swing if the mace procs. Callum's got an infection risk now and his DEX is going to drop when the hematoma reabsorbs. We've got *four minutes* of DPS before she closes the gap and eats you standing in the back, doc."

I looked at her. She looked at me. And I saw, in the way she held her bow, the particular exhaustion of a woman who'd been hitting numbers on a page for years and had just, for the first time, seen someone *read the sentence underneath.*

"I can't heal her," I said. "But I can tell you where to hit her so it *works.*"

Drenna smiled. It was not a warm smile. It was the smile of a woman who'd just found a new tool and wanted to see if it broke.

"Lead the way, doc."

---

The fourth chamber of the Gristle Hollow was a cathedral of chitin.

The walls were layered with the shed exoskeletons of gristles—thousands of them, translucent and amber, clicking softly in the draft from some unseen crack. The floor was a compost heap of beetle frass and old bones, and at the center of it, on a raised platform of fused carapace, the Matriarch waited.

She was the size of a horse. The system overlay painted her in clean, legible strokes:

> **Gristle Matriarch**
> **Level 7 — Elite**
> **HP: 4,200/4,200**
> **Resistances: Physical (Medium), Fire (High), Poison (Immune)**
> **Weakness: Blunt (Low)**
> **Drops: Gristle Chitin x2, Matriarch's Gland (quest item)**

Brask read it aloud, as was custom. The system's weakness tag said *Blunt (Low)*, which meant hitting her with the mace would do slightly less damage than hitting a normal gristle. A puzzle. The standard approach was to grind through her 4,200 HP over twenty minutes, manage your dodges, keep the healer topped up. A *balance encounter.* The system had designed her to be killed slowly, *correctly*, in a way that generated XP and loot and made the party feel like they'd *earned* it.

I didn't look at the blue box. I looked at *her.*

And I activated [???].

It didn't feel like casting a spell. It felt like *leaning in.* Like squinting at a monitor in a dark room until the pixels resolved. The blue overlay flickered, and for a half-second, I saw the Matriarch *without it.*

What I saw made me sit down on the beetle frass.

She was *dying.*

Not from us. Not from any wound. Her carapace was a patchwork of system-sealed breaches, dozens of them, hundreds, layered over each other like scar tissue on a burn victim. The system had kept her alive through encounters. Adventurers had hit her, cracked her, *broken* her, and the system had welded the pieces back together and reset her HP to 4,200 and said *she's fine, she's fine, she's fine, go again.*

But the welding was crude. The system could close a wound, could restore a number, could not *rebuild a thoracic ganglion.* Her left cluster was a mess of misfired nerve tissue. Her respiratory spiracles were partially fused shut. She couldn't turn properly to the right. Her abdomen was distended with old, system-managed hemolymph that should have drained three hundred encounters ago.

She was a corpse on life support. A Level 7 *boss fight* made of a thing that had died a long time ago and was being *kept running* by the only architecture this world had.

"Doc?" Drenna said. "You look like you've seen a ghost."

"I've seen a *patient,*" I said. And then, because I couldn't stop myself: "She's been dead for a very long time."

Brask snorted. "She's got four thousand HP, doc."

"She's got four thousand HP because the system *refuses to let her die.* Look at her left side. The third segment from the front. See the discoloration?"

Brask squinted. "Yeah. Brownish patch. System doesn't flag it."

"It's dead tissue. Encased. The system sealed over it so it wouldn't register as damage, but it's *necrotic.* The whole ganglion cluster under that plate is non-functional. She can't feel her left legs. She's been *dragging* them for who knows how long."

Drenna lowered her bow. "If she can't feel her left legs—"

"Then she can't brace against her left side," I said. "Brask. Forget the blunt damage bonus. Forget the system's weakness tag. I need you to hit her *left third segment.* The brownish patch. Hit it with everything you've got, and *do not stop* until the plate cracks."

Brask looked at the blue box. The box said *Blunt (Low).* The box said *hit the right side, where the system has helpfully painted a glowing weak-point marker.*

Brask looked at me.

He walked up to the Matriarch.

She turned to face him—slowly, wrong, her left legs dragging, her abdomen sagging, a sound like wet gravel in her thorax. The system painted her HP bar in clean green. *4,200/4,200.* *Fine. Fine. Fine.*

Brask swung the mace at the brown patch.

The plate didn't crack. It *shattered.* And beneath it, the ganglion came out in a spray of black fluid, and the Matriarch's entire left side *dropped,* and her HP bar, for the first time I'd seen, *flickered.* Not down. *Flickered.* Like a monitor losing signal. Like a number that didn't know what number to be.

"What the *hell*—" Callum started.

"Again!" I shouted. "Hit her again! Don't let the system re-seal it!"

Brask hit her again. And again. Drenna put an arrow through the gap in the shattered plate, angling down, *into the dead tissue.* The Matriarch shrieked—a sound that was not the system's *boss roar* audio file, but a wet, confused, *animal* sound, the sound of a thing feeling something it hadn't felt in a very long time.

Her HP dropped to 3,800. Then 3,100. Then the system tried to re-seal the wound, and I *felt* it, felt the architecture of the world reaching in to *fix the number,* to make her *fine,* to reset her to 4,200 and say *go again, go again, go again*—

And I reached into the [???] skill and *pulled.*

I don't know how to describe what I did. It wasn't a spell. There was no mana cost, no cooldown, no blue rectangle confirming the action. It was closer to *declining a consult.* To looking at the system's offer to heal the wound and saying *no, I'll handle it.*

The system's re-seal stalled. Hit the dead tissue. Found nothing to seal. *Pulled back.*

The Matriarch's HP dropped to 1,200.

Brask hit her a fourth time, and the third segment came off, and she fell.

She hit the floor of the chamber and did not get up. Her legs twitched. Her spiracles opened and closed, opening and closing, filling with air that her fused lungs couldn't use. The blue box above her read:

> **HP: 0/4,200**
> **Status: DECEASED**
> **+2,400 XP distributed**
> **Loot: Gristle Chitin x2, Matriarch's Gland (quest item)**

And the [???] skill, at the edge of my vision, showed me a second thing. A smaller text. A *footnote.*

> **Anatomical note: Subject expired 347 encounters ago. System-maintained state discontinued. Cause of death: natural. Cause of life: artificial.**

I sat down on the floor. My hands were shaking. Not from the fight. From the *number.* Three hundred and forty-seven adventurers had walked into this cave and *killed a corpse.* And the system had handed them XP. And they'd gone home and leveled up and said *good fight* and never once looked at the brown patch or the dragging legs or the wet, confused sound.

"Doc." Brask was standing over the body. His mace was dripping. "Doc, what the *hell* was that."

I looked up at him. At Drenna, who was staring at the Matriarch's body with an expression I couldn't read. At Callum, hand on his neck, thread still in the skin, alive because I'd done something the system said was unnecessary.

"That," I said, "was a patient who'd been dead for three hundred and forty-seven fights. And the system kept her running because the *encounter* needed a boss. Because the *XP table* needed a number. Because *you* needed something to hit so you could feel like you were *winning.*"

Silence. The beetles clicked. The dead Matriarch's spiracles ticked, slower, slower.

Drenna said, very quietly, "The Healer's Accord."

"What about them?"

"The Accord *built* the system, doc. Or commissioned it. God-knows-how-many years ago. They wrote the rules. They decided what HP meant. They decided what a *wound* was." She looked at me. "They're going to notice you saw the brown patch."

And as if on cue, the blue rectangle in my peripheral vision flickered. The red flag from the first chamber was still there, still pulsing. But beneath it, a new line of text had appeared. Not a system notification. Not a quest prompt. Something with a *signature.* A watermark. A tiny gear-and-caduceus logo I recognized from the pendant at my throat.

> **HEALER'S ACCORD — INTERDEPARTMENTAL MEMO**
> **Re: Anomalous practitioner, Velmara summons batch 4,417**
> **The subject performed an unsanctioned anatomical intervention on a system-maintained entity. This is a violation of the Healing Framework (Sections 1 through 4,000,000).**
> **The subject also demonstrated knowledge of pre-System anatomical taxonomy. This is not possible. The subject is not in the registry.**
> **Dispatch: Auditor Verain, Tier 3. ETA: 48 hours.**
> **Instruction to subject: Remain in Velmara. Do not perform further surgery. Do not speak to anyone about what you saw.**
> **The System says you are fine. Please act accordingly.**

I read it twice. I looked at Drenna. I looked at Brask, who was still holding his mace like a man who'd just been told the ground was a suggestion.

"Well," I said. "I suppose we should get back to town before the auditor arrives."

"And the surgery?" Brask asked. "The next one. When someone's HP is full but their *neck is filling with blood.*"

I looked at the [???] skill. Lv. 1. Unlearnable. The old doctor's way. The thing the system had no category for, because the system had been built by people who'd decided that *bodies* were a problem to be abstracted, not a reality to be tended.

"We do it again," I said. "And we do it *faster* than the auditor can get here."

Drenna shouldered her bow. Brask sheathed his mace. Callum touched his neck, where my thread held his vein shut, and said, very small, "Thanks, doc."

I nodded. I stood up. I picked up the Matriarch's Gland from the floor, where the system said it was a *quest item,* and where my fingers told me it was a *swollen, necrotic lymph node* that should have been removed three hundred fights ago.

I put it in my pack. I didn't know what I'd do with it.

I didn't know what I'd do with any of it.

But I knew the system was going to try to make me *fine.* And I knew, with the bone-deep certainty of a man who'd held four hundred hearts in his hands, that *fine* was the most dangerous word in any language.

We walked out of the cave into the dark. Behind us, the Matriarch's body went still. Really still. The way dead things go still when the machinery is finally, finally *off.*

And for the first time since I'd woken up in the dirt, the blue rectangles in my vision went quiet.

Not gone. Never gone.

But quiet.

Like a monitor holding its breath.

Chapter 2 end.
