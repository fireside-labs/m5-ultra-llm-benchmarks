**Chapter 2: The First Stone**

I didn't have time to be clever. I had time to be efficient.

The [Modify Terrain] menu opened before me like a blank canvas, and for the first time since I'd woken up in this nightmare, I felt a spark of something familiar. Something like joy. This was my element. This was what I did. I didn't fight monsters with swords or magic. I fought them with geometry and psychology.

My mana sat at 67. The adventurers were seconds from entering my room. I had a single, rectangular chamber with crumbling walls, a broken chest in the corner, and a mimic that had just eaten a goblin. That was my entire arsenal.

I needed to change the battlefield.

The interface was intuitive, almost as if it had been designed by someone who understood game mechanics. Probably because it had been. My own systems, my own logic, were baked into this world's fundamental rules. That thought was terrifying and exhilarating in equal measure.

I selected the floor directly in front of the entrance. The stone was old, cracked, and weak. Perfect for what I had in mind.

**[TERRAIN MODIFICATION]**

**Target: Floor Section (3m x 3m)**

**Action: Excavate**

**Depth: 4 meters**

**Cost: 20 Mana**

**Warning: Structural instability detected. Reinforce adjacent sections to prevent collapse.**

The stone beneath my feet shuddered, and I stumbled back as a section of the floor simply ceased to exist, collapsing into a dark pit. Dust billowed up, and I heard the distant clatter of rocks hitting the bottom. Four meters deep. Enough to break a leg, maybe worse if they landed wrong.

But a pit trap alone wouldn't kill a party of four. They'd just climb out, angry and alert. I needed more.

I had 47 mana left.

I selected the walls adjacent to the pit, reinforcing them with a thin layer of dense stone. The cost was 5 mana per section, and I applied it to the two walls flanking the entrance. The crumbling mortar seemed to solidify, the cracks sealing themselves. Structural integrity was still low, but it would hold.

42 mana remaining.

I looked at my blueprints. The mimic was still in the corner, lurking inside its chest disguise. It was a one-shot weapon, and it had already used its surprise advantage on the goblin. If the adventurers saw the chest, they'd be suspicious. I needed to reposition it.

The interface allowed me to issue simple commands to my minions. I sent a mental command to the mimic, ordering it to move. The chest scraped across the floor, shuffling to the side of the pit, near the wall. It positioned itself so that anyone who avoided the pit would naturally walk past it.

There. The beginning of a kill box.

But I still needed more. I needed something to soften them up before they even reached the pit.

The voices were closer now. Right outside the door.

"Alright, let's do this quick," the man's voice said. Barin, the woman had called him. "I want to be back at the inn before sundown. The stew there is fantastic."

"Focus," the woman replied. "I'm sensing something. There's a faint magical signature. Something's alive in there."

"Everything's alive in there. Mold, bugs, probably a rat or two. Let's just go."

The door creaked. It was a heavy, iron-banded thing, and it groaned as it swung inward. I pressed myself against the far wall, behind the mimic's chest, trying to make myself as small as possible. My body still ached from the mana infusion, and I could feel a cold sweat on my brow. I was a designer, not a fighter. If this went wrong, I was dead.

The first person through the door was a man in battered leather armor, a sword already drawn. He was tall and broad-shouldered, with a thick beard and a scar across his cheek. Barin. He had the look of a man who relied on brute force over finesse.

Behind him came a woman in a simple robe, a wooden staff clutched in her hands. She was younger, maybe mid-twenties, with sharp, intelligent eyes that swept the room immediately. The mage. She was the dangerous one. She'd sense the trap if I wasn't careful.

And behind her, two more. A dwarf with a heavy crossbow, and an elf with a bow, already nocked. A standard adventuring party. Tank, mage, ranged DPS, and another ranged DPS. They were Tier 1, but they knew how to work together.

Barin stepped into the room, his boots crunching on loose gravel. He stopped, scanning the space. His eyes passed over the pit, and I held my breath. The pit was dark, and the room was lit only by the flickering torches. It was a natural shadow. He wouldn't see it unless he was looking for it.

"Empty," he said, a note of disappointment in his voice. "I told you. This is a waste of time."

The mage stepped in behind him, her eyes narrowed. "No. There's something here. The magical signature is strong. It's coming from..."

She looked down. Right at the pit.

"Barin, stop!"

But it was too late. Barin took another step forward, and his foot found nothing but air. He let out a startled yelp as he pitched forward, his arms flailing. He vanished into the pit with a sickening crunch and a howl of pain.

"Barin!" the elf shouted, rushing forward. The dwarf followed, his crossbow raised.

The elf was fast, too fast. He reached the edge of the pit and skidded to a halt, peering down. "He's alive! His leg's broken, but he's alive!"

The dwarf knelt beside the elf, reaching down to help. They were both focused on the pit, both distracted.

And the mimic was right next to them.

I sent the command. *Now.*

The chest lunged. It moved with a speed that belied its wooden exterior, its lid snapping open like a jaw. The elf heard the sound and turned, his eyes widening in horror. He tried to raise his bow, but the mimic was faster. It clamped onto his arm, teeth sinking deep into flesh. The elf screamed, a high-pitched, agonized sound, and the mimic began to drag him toward the pit.

The dwarf reacted, swinging his crossbow like a club. He struck the mimic's side, and the wooden shell cracked, but the mimic didn't let go. It pulled the elf closer to the edge, and the elf's feet scrabbled uselessly against the stone.

The mage was chanting, her staff glowing with a pale blue light. A bolt of ice shot from the tip, striking the mimic square in the chest. The mimic shuddered, frost spreading across its wooden surface, but it still didn't release its grip.

I had to act. I couldn't just watch.

I looked at my mana. 42. I needed something cheap, something effective. I scrolled through my blueprints, my eyes scanning the list.

**[Create Minor Construct] (Cost: 15 Mana)**

**[Create Spike Trap] (Cost: 10 Mana)**

**[Create Acid Pool] (Cost: 20 Mana)**

**[Create Shadow Wisp] (Cost: 12 Mana)**

The shadow wisp. It was a weak, ethereal creature that could distract enemies, drain their stamina. It wasn't a killer, but it didn't need to be. It just needed to buy me time.

I selected it and confirmed the creation. My mana dropped to 30.

A wisp of dark energy materialized near the ceiling, coalescing into a vaguely humanoid shape. It was barely visible, a flicker of shadow against the torchlight. It drifted down, silent and insubstantial, and hovered behind the mage.

The mage was focused on the mimic, her staff glowing again. She didn't see the wisp until it was too late.

The wisp lunged, passing through her like a cold breeze. She gasped, her concentration breaking, and the spell fizzled. She stumbled forward, clutching her chest, and the wisp wrapped around her, draining her energy.

"What is this?" she shouted, her voice panicked. "There's something else here!"

The dwarf turned, abandoning the mimic, and raised his crossbow. He fired at the wisp, but the bolt passed harmlessly through its incorporeal form. The wisp shrieked, a sound like nails on a chalkboard, and dove at the dwarf.

Meanwhile, the mimic had dragged the elf to the edge of the pit. The elf's screams had become gurgles, and his struggles were weakening. The mimic gave one final, violent jerk, and the elf tumbled into the pit, landing on top of Barin with a wet thud. The screaming from below intensified.

I had two down. Two to go.

The dwarf was swatting at the wisp, his crossbow useless. The mage was recovering, her staff glowing again, but she was visibly weakened. Her movements were sluggish, her eyes unfocused.

I needed to press the advantage. I looked at my mana. 30. I could afford another trap, or another minion.

But I also needed to think about the long game. This was just the first party. The System had said there would be more. I couldn't spend everything on this one fight.

I compromised. I selected the spike trap and placed it directly beneath the mage's feet.

**[TRAP PLACED]**

**Spike Trap: A concealed panel that springs open, impaling anything above it.**

**Cost: 10 Mana**

**Mana Remaining: 20**

The floor beneath the mage shifted, almost imperceptibly. She didn't notice. She was too focused on the wisp, too focused on trying to cast her spell.

The wisp dove at her again, and she raised her staff to block it. The distraction was perfect.

I triggered the trap.

The panel snapped open with a sound like a gunshot, and a dozen iron spikes shot upward. The mage let out a choked gasp as the spikes pierced her legs, her robes, her torso. She hung there, impaled, her staff clattering to the ground. Her eyes were wide with shock, and then they went glassy.

The dwarf finally managed to strike the wisp with the butt of his crossbow, and the creature dispersed with a hiss. He turned, saw the mage's body, and let out a roar of fury.

"Elara! No!"

He charged, not at me, but at the pit, as if he could somehow save his companions. He was reckless, driven by rage. He didn't see the mimic, which had retreated to the shadows, its wooden shell cracked and splintered but still functional.

The mimic lunged again, but the dwarf was ready. He spun, swinging his crossbow, and caught the mimic in the side. The wooden shell shattered, and the mimic collapsed into a pile of broken planks and flesh, twitching weakly before going still.

The dwarf stood there, panting, his eyes wild. He was the last one standing. He looked around the room, searching for the source of his misery. His eyes landed on me, cowering in the corner.

"There you are," he growled, raising his crossbow. "You did this. You're the dungeon master."

I had 20 mana. I had no minions. I had no traps.

And I had a dwarf with a crossbow aiming at my head.

This was it. This was the moment I died.

But I was a game designer. I always had a backup plan.

I reached into my pocket—the tattered robes I'd woken up in—and pulled out a small, smooth stone. It wasn't anything special, just a pebble I'd picked up when I first woke up. But I held it up, and I smiled.

"You know," I said, my voice surprisingly steady, "in my game, there's a rule. Always check the loot before you kill the boss."

The dwarf blinked, confused. "What?"

I threw the pebble. It wasn't aimed at him. It was aimed at the torch on the wall above him.

The pebble struck the torch, and it fell, clattering to the ground. The oil from the torch spilled across the floor, igniting instantly. A wall of fire erupted between me and the dwarf, cutting him off.

He shouted, stumbling back, and in that moment of confusion, I turned and ran. I didn't have a plan. I didn't have a destination. I just ran, deeper into the dungeon, toward the core.

The fire wouldn't kill him. It would just slow him down. But that was all I needed. A few seconds of breathing room.

I reached the core chamber, a small, circular room with the damaged crystal at its center. I collapsed against the wall, gasping for breath, my heart pounding in my chest.

The System panel appeared in front of me.

**[SYSTEM NOTIFICATION]**

**Enemy Party: 4/4 Eliminated.**

**Mana +40.**

**Soul Essence +4.**

**New Blueprints Unlocked: [Elven Archer], [Dwarven Crossbowman].**

**Dungeon Expansion Available: 2 New Rooms.**

I stared at the notification, my mind racing. I had won. Barely. By the skin of my teeth. But I had won.

The dwarf was still alive, though. He was still coming. And I had no way to stop him.

But I had new blueprints. And I had new rooms.

I looked at the core, its cracks pulsing with a faint, purple light. I had 60 mana now. I had options.

The first room of my dungeon was a testament to survival. The second room would be a masterpiece.

I opened the blueprints and started to design.

The dwarf's footsteps echoed in the distance, growing closer.

I had minutes. Maybe less.

I smiled.

Let's build.
